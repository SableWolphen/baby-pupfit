🎯 Fastest sustainable route

The goal is not to do the most exercises. It is to recover from the highest-return hard sets, add reps or weight over time, and keep nutrition consistent. Glutes get the most weekly work, thighs support the silhouette, abs are trained directly, and upper-body volume stays moderate.

🍑 Glute priority🦵 Thick thighs✨ Abs 2–3×/wk⚙️ EOS machines📈 Double progression

⚡ Non-negotiables

Train hard, not sloppy
Most working sets finish with about 1–2 reps in reserve (RIR). Isolation work can occasionally reach 0–1 RIR with clean form.
Beat the logbook
Add a rep, improve control, or add the smallest weight jump whenever the rep target is achieved.
Full useful range
Use controlled depth that you can own without pain or your pelvis/back taking over.
Recover to grow
Keep at least ~48 hours between the two hardest lower-body sessions and sleep enough to perform.

📈 Exact progression system

Double progression: keep the same weight until you reach the top of the rep range on every working set with clean form and roughly 1–2 RIR. Then increase by the smallest available increment and build the reps back up.

Example — Hip Thrust 4 × 6–10
Week 1: 8/8/7/7 → Week 2: 9/8/8/7 → eventually 10/10/10/10 → add weight next workout.

If you miss your previous performance for 2 sessions in a row, do not automatically add more volume. Check sleep, food, soreness, technique, and recovery first.

🧠 Technique cues that matter most

Hip thrust
Ribs down, pelvis controlled, pause at the top, don't hyperextend your lower back.
Leg press / hack squat
Use the deepest pain-free range you can control. Knees track naturally with your feet.
Kickback
Don't turn it into a whole-body swing. Stable torso, hip does the work.
Abduction
Slow enough that the stack never slams; own both directions.

🔁 EOS machine swaps

Glute Drive missing: Smith-machine hip thrust. Hack squat missing: pendulum squat or leg press. Kickback missing: cable kickback. Seated curl missing: lying leg curl. Ab crunch missing: cable crunch or captain's chair.

🥩 Body-composition basics

Keep your main Baby PupFit nutrition targets consistent, prioritize protein, and use a moderate calorie deficit only if fat loss is part of the goal. The waist becomes leaner mainly through overall fat loss; the glutes and thighs change through months of progressive resistance training. Crash dieting can make scale weight fall faster while making muscle growth and training performance worse.

Keep daily movement consistent; the existing tracker step target is a good baseline. Adjust food or activity based on multi-week trends rather than a single weigh-in.

🛠️ Every 5–8 weeks

If joints feel beat up, motivation tanks, or performance is sliding despite good recovery, take a deload week: keep the same exercises, use roughly half the usual hard sets, and stay several reps away from failure. Then resume progression.

⏱️ What “fast” actually means

Visible changes can happen within weeks, but large glute/thigh growth and a dramatically different waist-to-hip ratio usually take months. Photos, posing, lighting, editing, and anatomy can exaggerate reference physiques. The fastest productive strategy is the one you can recover from and progressively overload.
