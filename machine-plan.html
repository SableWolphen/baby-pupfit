Setup, effort and progression

For EGYM, follow staff onboarding, range-of-motion calibration, strength testing and the resulting training resistance. Follow the installed program’s tempo, repetitions and retest prompts. The listed sets/reps are a conventional-machine/open-mode template; ask staff to adapt it if the circuit uses timed sets. Record displayed resistance only when meaningful, consistently in lb. No automatic EGYM load increases or hardcoded starting pounds.

For other machines, warm up for 5–10 minutes and do 1–3 progressively heavier practice sets before the first compound. Set seat/pads and pain-free range with staff. Working sets usually stop with 2–3 clean reps in reserve. Rest 2–3 minutes for compounds and 90–120 seconds for isolations; warm-ups do not count as working sets.

Start with 2 working sets per exercise for the first two weeks. Use Save & end early for reduced sessions; never mark unperformed sets done. Build to the base below only when soreness resolves between sessions and performance is steady. Add reps first. On conventional machines, when every prescribed set reaches the top of the range at the same load with at least 2 reps left, increase by the smallest available increment (roughly 2–5%). If the increment is too large, keep the load and improve control. Reduce if you cannot reach the lower rep target cleanly.

Saturday • Rest

No lifting. Gentle walking is optional. No make-up workouts.

Sunday • 3–5 mile run

Keep it conversational most weeks. Use the 3-mile end when recovering; avoid turning it into a weekly race. Monday is upper body so legs have more time before Tuesday. If pain changes your gait, stop rather than forcing the mileage.

Recovery governs speed

Wednesday is optional, low-fatigue accessory work. If sleep is poor, soreness persists beyond 48–72 hours, or performance drops for two comparable sessions, skip Wednesday and reduce the next session’s sets by about half. Use lighter resistance and 3–4 reps in reserve. If fatigue persists across several movements, deload for 5–7 days, then rebuild. Do not make up missed sets. Stop a painful movement and get help with persistent pain.

Protect 7–9 hours of sleep. Compare performance only on the same machine, setup, range and training mode; EGYM modes can change resistance profiles. The tracker’s recovery prompts are conservative clues, not a diagnosis.

Modest cut or recomp

Begin near maintenance if new to training or recovery is marginal; otherwise use roughly a 200–300 kcal/day deficit from your observed maintenance. Aim for about 0.25–0.5% bodyweight loss per week, or stable weight with a slowly shrinking waist and improving strength. No aggressive bulk or crash cut.

Protein: about 0.7–1.0 g/lb/day (1.6–2.2 g/kg). Keep carbs around training and the Sunday run. Compare calendar-week weight averages with at least 3 readings per week; measure waist weekly and photos every 2–4 weeks if useful. After 2–3 consistent weeks, adjust by only 100–150 kcal if needed. Stable weight with improving waist/strength is successful recomp. If weight falls over 0.5% weekly or strength, sleep or energy deteriorate, add food toward maintenance. Do not chase a tiny waist at the expense of health or performance.
